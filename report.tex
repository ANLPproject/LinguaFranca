\title{Do Internal Failures Predict External Ones?\\
Hidden-State Probes as Early Triggers for Repairing Multi-Hop RAG}

\author{Navadeep Kiran \quad Havish Balaga \quad Anish Racherla \quad Yogansh \\
  International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad}

\begin{abstract}
Small language models answering multi-hop questions often resolve an early reasoning step wrongly and then build a fluent answer on top of it; repair frameworks such as Doctor-RAG notice this only after the full answer is written, by asking a second model to read it. We test whether a linear probe on the generating model's own hidden states can flag a failing hop as it is written and trigger retrieval and a restart. On 2WikiMultihopQA with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, a layer-12 probe detects failing hops with AUROC 0.947 (hop 1) and 0.925 (hop 2), against at most 0.78 and 0.73 for zero- and few-shot LLM judges of up to 8B parameters and at most 0.68 for token-confidence signals; a 184M-parameter text diagnoser fine-tuned on the same labels matches it (0.959 vs.\ 0.954) but needs a separate model pass per check. The signal is present within the first three tokens of a hop. As a restart trigger the probe raises accuracy from 34.2\% to 53.8\% (59.4\% with two restarts), against 45.4\% for the matched judge; restarting after every second hop with no detector reaches 55.4\%, so the probe's advantage is selectivity (34\% fewer restarts, half the broken answers), not a higher ceiling. Activation patching shows that the probe reads whether a hop is grounded in the context and tracks interventions pair by pair, while the single hop-boundary state is not causal. An error budget and a retrieval ablation show that retrieval, not detection, is the remaining bottleneck.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets a language model answer questions by composing facts from retrieved documents \citep{lewis2020retrieval}. For multi-hop questions, small models fail in a characteristic way: they resolve an early hop wrongly, or skip it, and then build a fluent answer on top of the mistake. Repair frameworks such as Doctor-RAG \citep{jiao2026doctor} detect this only after the whole trajectory has been generated, by asking a second model to read the text. This is costly, since a full faulty answer is produced and then re-read, and indirect, since the diagnosis rests on text that can look equally fluent whether it is right or wrong.

We ask whether the generating model's own hidden states can flag a failing hop \emph{when the hop is written}. We train a logistic-regression probe on the mean hidden state of each hop, read it when the hop closes, and, if the score crosses a threshold, halt generation, retrieve evidence and restart. We compare it with LLM judges, a fine-tuned text diagnoser, token-confidence triggers and trivial triggers under matched conditions: same model, prompt and repair; only the decision to restart differs. Our research questions are:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{RQ1} Can a probe detect and localize a failing hop at its boundary?
\item \textbf{RQ2} Is the probed signal causally tied to the failure, or only correlated with it?
\item \textbf{RQ3} How does the trigger compare with text-based diagnosis in detection, localization, cost and post-repair accuracy?
\item \textbf{RQ4} At which layers does failure become decodable, and how robust are the conclusions to the choice of probe, threshold and baseline?
\end{itemize}

Our main findings are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item The probe detects failing hops far better than LLM judges and token-confidence signals (AUROC 0.947/0.925 vs.\ at most 0.78/0.73) and flags a failure after $\sim$54 generated tokens instead of 170. A text diagnoser fine-tuned on the same labels matches it, but needs a separate model pass; the probe reuses activations the generator already computed.
\item As a restart trigger it raises 2Wiki accuracy from 34.2\% to 53.8\% (+6.6 points on HotpotQA), +8.4 points over the matched judge (95\% CI [+5.0, +11.8]).
\item Always restarting after hop 2 matches this accuracy (55.4\%), and judges with accuracy-optimal thresholds tie it: the probe's advantage is selectivity, with 34\% fewer restarts and half the broken answers.
\item The probe reads whether the hop is grounded in the context, not its wording, and tracks interventions on the entity representation (within-pair $r{=}+0.59$), although a single hop-boundary state is not causally sufficient. The signal is decodable within the first three tokens of a hop.
\item The remaining errors are mostly retrieval failures (21.8\% of questions even under an oracle trigger), and a stronger retriever lifts the pipeline by 2.0 points; out of domain, detection is what degrades.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diagnosing and repairing RAG failures.} Doctor-RAG \citep{jiao2026doctor} diagnoses a completed trajectory with a fine-tuned second model and repairs from the earliest failing step; Self-RAG \citep{asai2024selfrag} trains the generator to critique its own retrievals, and IRCoT \citep{trivedi2023interleaving} interleaves retrieval with chain-of-thought steps. Process reward models \citep{lightman2024lets} score each step but need a separately trained verifier and operate on text. We keep the repair simple, vary only the trigger, and compare against both LLM judges and a diagnoser fine-tuned on our labels.

\paragraph{When to retrieve.} FLARE \citep{jiang2023active} retrieves when the model emits a low-probability token and DRAGIN \citep{su2024dragin} uses token entropy. Probing-RAG \citep{baek2025probing} probes hidden states, but at the level of a whole query, to decide whether another retrieval round is needed. We probe individual hops and compare against FLARE-style and entropy signals on the same hops, for detection and end to end.

\paragraph{Probing internal states.} Hidden states of language models encode whether a statement is true or whether the model knows the answer \citep{azaria2023internal,kadavath2022language,burns2023discovering}. We follow standard probing practice, with linear probes \citep{alain2017understanding}, grouped splits, shuffled-label controls \citep{hewitt2019designing} and caution about what a probe's accuracy shows \citep{belinkov2022probing}.

\paragraph{Causal analysis.} Activation patching and causal tracing \citep{meng2022locating,vig2020investigating,zhang2024towards} test whether a representation is sufficient to restore a behaviour. We use them on minimal pairs to ask where the information a hop depends on is processed and whether the probe follows it.

\section{Task and Data}

\paragraph{Questions and context.} We use 2WikiMultihopQA \citep{ho2020constructing}, whose gold reasoning graph gives the entity each hop should state. We filter it to true two-step compositional chains (excluding comparison questions) and sample 4,000 questions (seed 42). Each comes with 10 paragraphs (2 gold, 8 distractors); to mimic a weak first-stage search, TF-IDF keeps the top 5. Both gold paragraphs survive for only 41\% of questions: the question names only the hop-1 entity, so TF-IDF almost always keeps the hop-1 paragraph and drops the hop-2 one. Of the 500 evaluation questions, 203 keep both, 282 miss the hop-2 paragraph, 6 miss the hop-1 paragraph and 9 miss both.

\paragraph{Model and format.} Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct \citep{dubey2024llama} (fp16, greedy, at most 400 new tokens) writes \texttt{<hop1>}\ldots\texttt{</hop1>}, \texttt{<hop2>}\ldots\texttt{</hop2>} and \texttt{<answer>}, which gives exact token boundaries for pooling.

\paragraph{Hop labels.} A hop is labelled a failure if its text does not state that hop's gold entity. The check runs from cheap to expensive: normalized string match, a Wikidata alias table, sentence-embedding similarity $\geq 0.70$ \citep{reimers2019sentence}, and an LLM judge as last resort. Hops beyond the two gold ones are unlabelled. On the held-out split, hop 1 fails in 17.4\% of cases and hop 2 in 65.0\%; 10.8\% of failing hops are hedges (``there is no information about \ldots''), the rest confident wrong or off-target statements. Only 19\% of hop-2 failures follow a failed hop 1, so error compounding explains little (Appendix~\ref{app:labels}, which also audits the labeler: under 0.5\% label noise).

\paragraph{Splits and evaluation sets.} A grouped 80/20 split gives 3,200 training and 800 held-out questions (6,325 / 1,586 labelled hops). Layers, hyper-parameters and thresholds are chosen by 5-fold grouped cross-validation inside the training split. End-to-end systems are evaluated on 500 held-out 2Wiki questions and on 500 HotpotQA \citep{yang2018hotpotqa} validation questions (404 bridge, 96 comparison) as an out-of-domain test. The retrieval pool has 94,031 paragraphs.

\section{Method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/pipeline.pdf}
\caption{The probe-triggered restart system. When a hop closes, the probe scores the pooled layer-12 hidden state. If $P(\text{fail})<\tau$ generation continues; otherwise it halts, retrieves with the hop text, and restarts from scratch with the evidence prepended. All compared systems share the model, prompt and repair; only this decision differs.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Probe.} Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} gives an overview. The feature is the mean of the block-$L$ hidden states over the tokens of a hop (3,072 dimensions). The probe is a standardizer followed by class-balanced logistic regression with $C{=}0.01$, one shared probe for both hops (dedicated probes are an ablation), at layer 12 on the cross-validation plateau (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Scores are Platt-calibrated \citep{platt1999probabilistic} (ECE 0.019 on the held-out split), and the trigger threshold $\tau{=}0.650$ maximizes $F_{0.5}$ on out-of-fold training predictions; $F_{0.5}$ favours precision, to avoid pointless restarts.

\paragraph{Live system.} Generation runs token by token with a KV cache and forward hooks that copy each block's output. When \texttt{</hopK>} ($K{=}1,2$) is generated, the hop's tokens are pooled and scored (about 9.5\,ms); if $P(\text{fail})\geq\tau$ generation halts. Features agree with those used to train the probe (cosine 0.99999).

\paragraph{Repair.} The retrieval query is the question plus the failing hop's text; for hop 2 the bridge entity from hop 1 comes first, because a failing hop 2 is often a hedge that does not name what to look up. The pool holds 13,719 paragraphs from the 4,000 questions, 4,871 from the HotpotQA evaluation questions and 75,441 from 20,000 other 2Wiki questions. We embed with MiniLM \citep{reimers2019sentence}, search exactly with FAISS \citep{johnson2019billion}, re-rank the top 20 with a cross-encoder \citep{nogueira2019passage}, and prepend the top 3 paragraphs not already in the prompt. The answer is then regenerated from scratch (``Naive Restart''); a restart that is flagged again triggers at most one more. If generation hits the token cap without an answer, the answer is forced from hops 1--2 (105 first answers, 441 restarts), for every system alike. The pool contains the evaluation questions' gold paragraphs, as a real Wikipedia index would.

\paragraph{Systems compared.} All share the model, prompt and repair: (i) no intervention; (ii) always retrieve with the question before generating; (iii) a judge trigger: after the full answer the same 3B model is asked, zero-shot, whether each hop is correct, with $P(\text{fail})$ from the yes/no logits and the earliest failing hop repaired; we also run Qwen2.5-3B/7B \citep{yang2024qwen25} and Llama-3.1-8B judges, and a two-shot 8B judge with one failing and one correct demonstration from the training split; (iv) a \emph{fine-tuned text diagnoser}, the analogue of Doctor-RAG's trained diagnoser: a DeBERTa-v3-base cross-encoder \citep{he2023debertav3} (184M parameters) fine-tuned on the probe's training rows and labels, reading the question, the earlier step, the step to check and the context (at most 512 tokens), with epoch and threshold chosen on 10\% of the training questions; (v) always restart after hop 2 (or hop 1); (vi) a hedge-keyword trigger; (vii) TF-IDF and logistic regression on the hop text, trained on the probe's rows; (viii) a random trigger at the probe's restart rate; (ix) token-confidence triggers (maximum and mean token negative log-likelihood as in FLARE, maximum and mean entropy as in DRAGIN) on the same hops, each with its own training-split threshold; (x) an oracle that restarts at the first truly failing hop, an upper bound for any trigger.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} An answer is correct if its token F1 with the gold answer is $\geq 0.5$ (exact match is also reported). Greedy decoding is deterministic, so a halted answer is an exact prefix of the full one; every system and threshold is therefore computed from cached generations and restarts, and 100 questions are also run fully live (same trigger and restart count for all 100, same correctness for 99). Confidence intervals are paired bootstraps over questions (2,000 resamples).

Table~\ref{tab:detect} shows that the probe separates failing from correct hops far better than every LLM judge, whatever its size, and than every token-confidence signal; an LR on all five confidence features reaches 0.731, and adding them to the probe does not help (0.953). Where FLARE stays silent (958 hops), the probe still reaches AUROC 0.958 and flags 259 of the 334 failures FLARE misses. The probe localizes the first failing hop in 74.4\% of failing trajectories (judge 24.8\%), is far better calibrated (ECE 0.024 against 0.22--0.48 for the judges on live hops), and fires after 54 generated tokens on average, against 170 for the judge, which must wait for the full answer. At the deployed threshold it misses 40\% of failing hop-1 and 15\% of hop-2 steps; 70--80\% of its false alarms occur on trajectories whose final answer is wrong anyway.

\paragraph{Supervision, not size or prompting, closes the gap.} The fine-tuned diagnoser matches the probe on the test split (difference $-0.005$ [$-0.013$, $+0.004$]; confident-failure recall 0.81 vs.\ 0.79; score correlation $\rho{=}0.85$). The failure is thus recoverable from text and context given supervision; the judges fail for lack of it. In-context examples are no substitute: the two-shot 8B judge is slightly worse than zero-shot (0.547 / 0.678 vs.\ 0.597 / 0.729; ECE 0.333 vs.\ 0.264). The probe's edge over a trained diagnoser is cost: each diagnoser check is a separate 184M-parameter pass over about 370 tokens (37.8\,ms on a T4) after a GPU fine-tuning run, whereas the probe is one dot product on states the generator has already computed and trains in seconds.

\paragraph{The probe trigger helps, and beats the judges.} On 2Wiki (Table~\ref{tab:e2e}) the probe gains +0.196 [+0.156, +0.236] over no intervention and beats the same-model judge by +0.084 [+0.050, +0.118]; with up to two restarts it reaches 0.594, above every other system including the one-restart oracle (0.550). Exact match gives the same ranking (0.406 vs.\ 0.258 for no intervention and 0.326 for the judge). On HotpotQA it gains +0.066 [+0.034, +0.098] and leads the same-model judge by +0.044 [+0.010, +0.078], but not the 7B judge (+0.024 [$-0.004$, +0.056]). Token-confidence triggers land at the judges' level on 2Wiki (0.454--0.456, about 0.08 below the probe, intervals above zero). The fine-tuned diagnoser ties the probe (2Wiki 0.534, $+0.004$ [$-0.016$, $+0.024$]; HotpotQA 0.570 for both; every matched-budget difference n.s.), though on HotpotQA it needs more restarts (0.72 vs.\ 0.59). The probe also costs less than the judge: 1,574 processed tokens per question against 2,576.

\paragraph{But a detector is not strictly needed for accuracy.} Restarting after every hop 2 reaches 0.554 on 2Wiki (probe $-0.016$ [$-0.038$, +0.006]) and 0.586 on HotpotQA, restarting 98\% of questions instead of 64\% and breaking twice as many answers (20 vs.\ 10). A random trigger at the probe's rate reaches only 0.465 (below the probe in all 200 seeds) and the text classifier 0.516. Most of the gain therefore comes from the repair, because hop 2 fails 65\% of the time. What the probe adds is selectivity: at matched restart budgets of 40--70\% it beats every judge on 2Wiki with all intervals above zero (e.g.\ at 60\%: 0.524 vs.\ 0.456--0.504; Figure~\ref{fig:budget}), and it matches always-restarting with 34\% fewer restarts.

\paragraph{Fair thresholds and robustness.} Given the same accuracy-optimal threshold procedure as the probe, judges collapse to restarting 93--99\% of questions and tie it (probe 0.554, judges 0.518--0.558). We therefore do \emph{not} claim a higher maximum accuracy than the judges: the maximum is set by always restarting. On 2Wiki the probe beats the judge at every $\tau$ from 0.30 to 0.90, conclusions are unchanged when forced answers count as wrong, and the probe also verifies restarts (restarts it still flags are 26\% accurate, those it clears 72\%).

\paragraph{HotpotQA.} The probe was trained on 2Wiki bridge chains. On the 404 bridge questions it gains +0.079 [+0.042, +0.119] over no intervention and +0.052 [+0.015, +0.089] over the judge; on the 96 comparison questions it gains nothing (+0.010 [$-0.062$, +0.083]). With automatic hop labels for bridge questions (Appendix~\ref{app:hotpot}), hop-1 AUROC drops to 0.738, still above the judges (at most 0.64), and hop-2 AUROC (0.675) only ties them (0.60--0.67) and token entropy (0.70). There an oracle trigger reaches 0.628 against the probe's 0.564 (none 0.479): out of domain the probe captures 57\% of the oracle's gain, against 94\% on 2Wiki, so what degrades is detection, not the repair.

Detection costs the probe 13.4\% of questions and the judge 38.0\%, but even a perfect trigger loses 21.8\% to retrieval (Table~\ref{tab:budget}); a restart that retrieves a missing gold paragraph is right about 72\% of the time, otherwise 12--13\%. Hop-2 restarts raise accuracy from 0.189 to 0.544 (101 fixed, 9 broken, $n{=}259$) and find a missing gold paragraph in 53\% of cases; hop-1 restarts raise it from 0.049 to 0.148 ($n{=}61$) and find it in 25\%. The hop-1 paragraph is present in 97\% of questions, so hop-1 failures are mostly misreadings of evidence already there (51\% restate the question's subject, 41\% copy a distractor's entity), which retrieval cannot repair. A retrieval ablation confirms the bottleneck: changing only the restart's retriever to a fusion of dense and BM25 rankings (cross-encoder re-ranking of the top 30) finds the missing hop-2 paragraph in 66\% of restarts instead of 53\% and raises the probe pipeline on 2Wiki from 0.538 to 0.558 (0.604 with two restarts; broken answers 10 to 5), with no gain on HotpotQA (0.550).

\subsection{What does the probe read? (RQ2)}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/causal_figure.pdf}
\caption{Causal analysis on 183--211 minimal pairs. (a) Recovery of the clean hop-1 log-probability when the clean states of a position group are patched into the counterfactual run, by block; the entity information moves from the entity tokens to the following tokens and is handed off around block 14. (b) Fraction of behaviour and of the probe's score restored when the entity span is patched; the probe reads block 12, so it lags behaviour. (c) Pair by pair, the more behaviour is restored, the more the probe's score falls (within-pair $r{=}+0.59$; old probe $+0.28$).}
\label{fig:causal}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Design.} Minimal pairs swap the question's entry entity for another entity of the same token length, so clean and counterfactual (CF) prompts differ only in that entity (219 held-out pairs; 211 informative, where the clean run states the gold hop-1 entity and the CF run does not). CFs are used only here, never for training. We copy the clean run's block-$L$ states into the CF run at one position group at a time, for every block: the entity span, its last token, the tokens after it to the end of the prompt (``tail''), the last prompt token, and the single state at \texttt{</hop1>}. We read out generation (does hop 1 now name the gold entity?) and logit causal tracing (the fraction of the clean-vs-CF log-probability gap of the clean hop 1 a patch recovers; 183 pairs with a gap of at least one nat). Controls patch a third entity's states into the same positions, any entity or one from the same question template. Generation outcomes are reproducible (0 of 3,066 differ between runs); full tables are in Appendix~\ref{app:causal}.

\paragraph{Where the information is processed.} Patching the entity span restores hop 1 in 100\% of pairs at block 0, 90\% at 8, 73\% at 10, 45\% at 12, 19\% at 14 and 8\% at 24. The third-entity control restores 6--16\%, so the effect is entity-specific through block 13 (entity minus control +0.24 at block 13, sign test $p{<}10^{-12}$; +0.07 at 14; n.s.\ by 24); against a same-template donor it stays significant through block 12 (+0.43, $p{=}0.03$, 14 pairs). The tail carries the information over blocks 9--13 (up to 0.50), but entity and tail together restore only 0.71--0.77 there, and from block 14 neither suffices (0.30 together). In logit tracing (Figure~\ref{fig:causal}a) the entity's last token peaks at 0.63 at block 7; MLPs at the entity ($\pm$2 tokens) contribute 0.38--0.40 at blocks 0--2 and 0.30--0.36 at 7--11, and nothing from block 16; and a sharp hand-off between blocks 13 and 14 (entity span 0.48 to 0.08, last prompt token 0.13 to 0.29) moves information to the last prompt token, which reaches 0.30 of the log-probability gap but restores at most 7\% in generation. This layout replicates our earlier study with all ten paragraphs (Table~\ref{tab:tracing}), except for the MLP profile.

\paragraph{A single hop-boundary state is not causal.} Overwriting only the state at \texttt{</hop1>} restores the answer in at most 1\% of pairs at blocks 8--20, so we do \emph{not} show that the probed boundary state alone causes the failure.

\paragraph{The probe follows the intervention and reads grounding, not wording.} On generated hops the tuned probe flags 0.5\% of clean and 69\% of CF runs (AUROC 0.990; old probe 0.935), and for patches below its own layer its verdict agrees with whether the entity was restored in 95\% of runs. With the hop-1 text held fixed (teacher-forced from the clean run) and only the question's entity changed, it still separates clean from CF with AUROC 0.960 (old probe 0.911), on identical words; patching the true entity lowers its score by 2.80 log-odds [2.67, 2.94] more than patching a third entity, in 99\% of pairs. Pair by pair, the behaviour a patch restores predicts the probe score it restores (within-pair, within-block $r{=}+0.59$ [+0.54, +0.64]; old probe $+0.28$; Figure~\ref{fig:causal}c). The probe also follows the third-entity control ($r{=}+0.37$), so it tracks how well the context supports the hop under any intervention, and it lags behaviour because it reads block 12 (a block-10 patch restores 0.78 of behaviour but 0.38 of its score).

\paragraph{Causal alignment chooses the layer.} Retrained at blocks 10 and 12 ($C$ from 0.001 to 1, five bootstrap samples), the probes have equal natural AUROC (0.92--0.94) but differ in alignment ($r$ 0.51--0.62 at block 12 vs.\ 0.20--0.46 at block 10), and $C{=}0.01$ aligns at least as well as $C{=}1$ in all ten comparisons, independently supporting the deployed probe. A direction estimated from CF pairs separates held-out CFs perfectly but natural failures only weakly (AUROC 0.57--0.72), whereas the natural probe transfers to CFs (0.96), which is why we do not train on CFs.

\subsection{Beyond hop-end detection}

(i) \emph{The signal is early.} A probe trained on partial hops reaches AUROC 0.834 after the first 10\% of a hop (about three tokens), 0.885 at half and 0.929 at the end, although 95\% of failing and 99\% of correct hops open with the identical words ``From the context, I find'' and hedges are only 11\% of failures; token-confidence signals on the same prefixes stay at or below 0.68. (ii) \emph{Stopping mid-hop keeps accuracy.} A hybrid trigger that stops a hop early when the partial-hop probe is very confident, and otherwise checks at \texttt{</hopK>}, keeps detection quality ($F_{0.5}$ 0.871 vs.\ 0.878) and stops 60\% of caught failures early. End to end, with every changed restart re-retrieved and regenerated, it reaches 0.554 on 2Wiki (+0.016 [$-0.004$, $+0.036$]) and 0.562 on HotpotQA (n.s.); the shorter query finds a missing gold paragraph as often as the full one (58.8\% vs.\ 56.4\%). It saves 7\% of generated tokens but not total compute, and over-fires out of domain. (iii) At the end of hop 1, the hidden state predicts that hop 2 will fail with AUROC 0.82, against 0.62 for the hop-1 text and 0.72 for a rule using the gold titles. (iv) For selective answering (abstaining on the highest failure scores), the probe's area under the risk-coverage curve is 0.418 against 0.528--0.615 for the judges (lower is better); answering the half of the questions it is most confident about, the probe pipeline is 74.8\% accurate, against 54.4\% for the matched judge.

\section{Discussion}

The probe works as a detector, and detection is no longer the limiting step. It reads something real (whether the hop is grounded in its context), is cheap, calibrated and early, and chooses restarts better than any LLM judge or confidence signal we tried. A diagnoser fine-tuned on the same labels does as well, which locates the probe's advantage: not information the text lacks, but a trained diagnoser's accuracy from states the generator already computes, during generation, with a causally checked signal. With this repair, restarting after hop 2 almost always is as accurate, the probe obtains 94\% of the oracle trigger's gain, and the remaining errors come from retrieval and hop-1 misreadings. In deployment terms the probe buys the same accuracy with fewer restarts and half the broken answers, rather than a higher ceiling.

\section{Conclusion}

A linear probe on a language model's hidden states detects failing reasoning hops at the hop boundary far better than LLM judges and token confidence, and as well as a fine-tuned text diagnoser at a fraction of its cost; it localizes the first failure, carries its signal from the first tokens of a hop, and as a restart trigger yields large, significant gains with fewer, better-chosen restarts. Activation patching shows that the probe tracks the grounding of the hop, while the boundary state alone is not sufficient. Honest controls attribute most of the end-to-end gain to the repair, and an error budget and a retrieval ablation identify retrieval as the bottleneck in domain and detection out of domain.

\section*{Limitations and Future Work}

All experiments use one generator (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct), two-hop questions from one in-domain dataset, and a simulated first-stage retriever; the retrieval pool contains the evaluation questions' own paragraphs, which is easier than open-web search. The judges are prompted LLMs (zero-shot, plus one two-shot variant), and the trained text diagnoser is a single model size trained with one seed. The repair was designed after seeing early 2Wiki results on the same evaluation questions, so HotpotQA is the cleaner test. A hop counts as failed when it does not state the gold bridge entity, which includes detours as well as errors; HotpotQA hop labels are derived automatically. The answer metric (token F1 $\geq 0.5$) is lenient on reformatted dates, which is why exact match is also reported. The counterfactual pairs used for causal analysis are an induced failure type, mostly hedges. Probe-model differences rest on one split and one MLP seed, and about 60 paired comparisons were made without correction for multiple testing.

Future work should replicate the study on other generators and datasets, train probes across datasets to improve out-of-domain detection, improve repair retrieval and the repair of hop-1 misreadings, test whether the probe and a trained diagnoser make complementary errors, and design restarts that reuse a partial hop instead of regenerating from scratch.

\section{Data and Label Quality}
\label{app:labels}

\paragraph{Data.} 4,000 questions with 8,000 gold hops; 89 hops the model never generated are excluded, leaving 6,325 training and 1,586 held-out hop rows. End-to-end evaluation uses 500 held-out 2Wiki and 500 HotpotQA questions.

\paragraph{Failure by evidence.} On the held-out split, hop 2 fails after a correct hop 1 in 126 of 303 cases (41.6\%) with both gold paragraphs present and in 284 of 343 (82.8\%) with only the hop-1 paragraph; only 19\% of hop-2 failures follow a failed hop 1. TF-IDF keeps both gold paragraphs for 40.4\% of held-out questions, only the hop-1 paragraph for 56.2\%, only the hop-2 paragraph for 1.2\% and neither for 2.1\%.

\paragraph{Labeler audit.} The Wikidata alias table contributed no matches in the TF-IDF labels (27 with all ten paragraphs). Looking up all 1,405 relevant gold entities in Wikidata would flip 0 of 1,167 audited failure labels (all-10) and 2 of 2,053 (TF-IDF); a date rule flips 13 and 1. Label noise from these sources is well under 0.5\%.

\paragraph{Answer metric.} On the 4,000 baseline answers, exact match is 26.2\% and F1 $\geq 0.5$ is 35.0\%. Of the 351 answers counted correct without an exact match, 77 are fair date reformattings, 192 mostly fair (``Moscow, Russia'' for Moscow) and 82 too lenient (a year for a full date, or a wrong month), inflating baseline accuracy by about two points. Exact match ranks all systems identically.

\section{Further Results}
\label{app:hotpot}

\paragraph{HotpotQA hop labels.} For bridge questions, hop 1 must name the bridge entity (the supporting paragraph whose title is not in the question; 234 of 404 questions are unambiguous) and hop 2 must contain the answer, checked with the 2Wiki matcher. On 2Wiki both rules agree with the LLM-checked labels on 97.3\% and 97.0\% of cases, erring towards ``fail''. Missed failing hops cost the probe 13.2\% of these questions and the judge 31.6\%; retrieval misses cost 4.7\%.

\paragraph{Calibration and answer selection.} ECE on live 2Wiki hops: probe 0.024, judges 0.221--0.482 (Brier 0.080 vs.\ 0.249--0.487). Choosing among the original answer and the two restarts by the lowest failure score gives 0.550 with the probe, 0.506 with the judge and 0.462 at random (oracle 0.626).

\paragraph{Case study.} \emph{``When was the performer of song Doesn't Really Matter born?''} (gold: May 16, 1966). Hop 1, ``the performer is Janet Jackson'', is correct (probe 0.04, judge 0.00). Hop 2, ``Janet Jackson is an American singer'', is fluent and confident but answers a different question: the probe flags it (0.90), the judge does not (0.07), and the first answer is ``American''. The probe halts at hop 2, retrieval brings the \emph{Janet Jackson} page, and the restart answers ``May 16, 1966''. 21 questions follow this pattern; conversely, 10 restarts break a correct answer, typically when the model had recovered from a failing hop on its own and the restart retrieved the wrong page.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Code, notebooks and results are in the project repository (the probe-suite comparison of Section~\ref{sec:design} on branch \texttt{experiment/distractor-rag}); hidden states, data and trained probes are on the Hugging Face dataset \texttt{AnishRacherla/LinguaFranca-Phase3}. Generation is greedy with fp16 weights; probes use scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011scikit} with fixed seeds. The pipeline runs on one Kaggle T4 GPU in about four hours for the 1,000 evaluation questions; probe training and cached-generation evaluations run on CPU.
